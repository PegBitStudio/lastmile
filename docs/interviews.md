## 1. Dami — own experience

### When the customer is not home

It depends on the kind of home.

- **Unfenced, unsecured homes.** The driver knocks on the front door. If nobody answers, the
  driver takes the parcel back and it is re-delivered later.
- **Gated homes and secure estates.** The driver can leave the parcel with the security guard or
  at the estate gate.
- **High-value items and electronics.** The driver calls the customer first. The customer either
  says to leave it with security, or asks the driver to come back.

### Who the driver contacts

The driver usually calls **the customer**, not the office.

- **Most services:** no app step. The driver phones the customer and gets spoken permission to
  hand the parcel to someone else, such as a colleague.
- **Some services:** the customer and the person collecting for them must both be registered in
  the app, and linked beforehand. The driver checks the right person on screen.

### When it goes wrong

- Failed deliveries happen, especially food. They are usually **driver error**, not a wrong
  address.
- The customer complains to the company. The company contacts the driver to sort it out.
- **Who pays, and how anyone proves what happened, is unclear.**

### Money

- Many deliveries use **independent couriers**, hired per job, price agreed each time.
- The delivery fee is paid to the driver, often **in cash**, by the seller or the buyer.
- Paying for the goods depends on the seller: **upfront**, or **pay on delivery**.

### Phones and voice

- Most drivers **do not use voice apps**. They use text messages and tap to confirm in an app.
- Calls are mainly to tell the customer "I'm nearby" or "I'm here".
- Some drivers have **smartphones** with delivery apps. Others have **basic phones** for calls
  only.

### Who pays for it

- **The dispatcher or the company owner** would pay. Not the driver.

### When a parcel goes missing

- The company should have a policy for it.
- **Sometimes the driver pays, sometimes the company pays.** It depends.

### Talking instead of typing

- Talking to the phone would be **much easier than typing** on the device.
